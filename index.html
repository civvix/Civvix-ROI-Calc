    Method & authorities

    1. The nine counties without return counts — Chester, Davidson, Hamilton, Hickman, Knox, Montgomery, Rutherford, Shelby and Williamson — default to the 86-county pooled state average: $83,876 assessed and $279,585 appraised per return, at the state average rate of 1.8169 per $100 (Davidson uses its verified 2.782 GSD rate). Their three-year assessed totals are real Tax Aggregate figures; only the per-return split is assumed. On the map they shade like any other county and carry a thin dark edge to mark them as estimated.
    2. County data. Return counts: Tennessee Comptroller of the Treasury, response to a Tennessee Public Records Act request, "Schedules Filed Per County" (86 counties × 3 years). Assessed values: State Board of Equalization, Tax Aggregate Report of Tennessee, Table I, "Total Personal Property Assessment" county-total row, 2023, 2024 and 2025 — locally assessed personal property only, excluding real property and state-assessed public utility property. County tax rates: UT CTAS County Profile System, corrected for the CTAS fiscal-year offset. Figures are pooled: three-year total dollars divided by three-year total returns, counting a year only where both a return count and an assessed value exist.
    3. Assessment ratio is a statewide statutory constant, not a county variable. Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-5-901(a)(2): "Industrial and commercial property shall be assessed at thirty percent (30%) of its value." The assessed-to-appraised ratio is exactly 0.30000 for all 86 reporting counties in the source data. What varies county to county is the tax rate — 0.9028 to 3.3610 per $100 of assessed value, median 1.8272.
    4. Non-filer sizing. The county average is the average tax per filed return, and filed returns include large manufacturers and distribution centres. A business discovered as a non-filer is smaller by selection. The sizing slider scales the county average down accordingly; at the 30% default a discovered non-filer is assumed to hold roughly a third of the average filer's assessable property. This is a judgement, not a measurement — it is the single largest source of uncertainty in the model and the first number a county's finance staff should be invited to change.
    5. The tax figure is a floor. Tennessee has no separate TPP rate; the county rate applies to all property in the county. Property inside an incorporated city also pays that city's rate, and in some counties a special school or fire district rate. 22 counties levy more than one countywide rate and are flagged on selection; for those the base rate outside cities and special districts is used. The uplift slider is how you model the stacked rates, and it defaults to 1.00× — i.e. off.
    6. Back assessment multiplies the annual tax by the years-recovered input. § 67-1-1005(a) permits reaching three years where there is a taxpayer reporting failure, fraud, or collusion, and tolls the deadline during a TPP audit. Penalty applies to back-assessed taxes only: § 67-1-1008 allows the board to impose "a penalty not to exceed fifteen percent (15%) of the taxes due" for, among other things, "failure to file a personal property schedule." Discretionary; 15% is a ceiling.
    7. License fees. $15 to the county, plus $15 to the city where the municipality levies the business tax (§ 67-4-723(a)(1), (b)(1)). The fee is a fee, not a tax: § 67-4-724 distributes only the § 67-4-704 state tax, the § 67-4-705 municipal tax and the § 67-4-710 fee, so the license fee never reaches the Department and the county keeps all of it. A standard license carries the fee once — § 67-4-723(a)(2), "There shall be no fee charged for the renewal of a license issued under this subsection (a)" — while a minimal activity license has no such clause and is repurchased annually (§ 67-4-723(b)(1), (b)(3); DOR Business Tax Manual p. 32; notice BUS-15). Hence the recurring license line.
    8. No back license fees are modelled, because none exist. Part 7 prohibits unlicensed operation (§ 67-4-723(a)(4)) but attaches no penalty, and § 67-4-716 extends the Chapter 1, Part 8 penalty machinery only to "taxes collected and administered by the commissioner" — which the clerk-collected fee is not. License-side compliance is prospective only.
    9. No business tax is credited either, which makes the model conservative. § 67-4-724(c) sweeps to the state general fund "one hundred percent (100%) of the amount of any tax, interest, and penalty assessed by the commissioner... as a result of an audit." Both limits matter: assessed by the commissioner, and from an audit. A business prompted into voluntary compliance that simply starts filing is neither, so the county would receive its ordinary distribution under § 67-4-724(a). None of that upside appears here.
    10. Mailing cost is charged against the county, at the per-letter rate you set. "By violation" sends one letter per violation actually present — the Assessor's for a TPP non-filer, the Clerk's for an unlicensed business, both for a dual violator. "Flat 2" charges two letters for every acted-on lead regardless. Letters are treated as a one-time discovery cost and do not recur.
    11. Assessed value is not revenue. The Tax Aggregate Report publishes an assessment base, not collections. Actual collections rest with county trustees and differ from billed amounts through delinquency, appeals and prorations. The "county acts & collects" input is where that friction lives.
    12. Compensation basis. Every fee here is a fixed dollar amount per record, invariant to assessed value, tax assessed or collected, and whether the Assessor acts. No output of this model feeds any fee. Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-5-507(c)(2) voids any contract in which compensation is "conditioned upon or measured directly or indirectly by an increase or a reduction in assessments" — this is a proposal aid, not a payment formula.
